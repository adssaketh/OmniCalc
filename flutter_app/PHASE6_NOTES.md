# Phase 6: Chemistry (master prompt Part 3)

Phase 6 is complete with this step. Everything here is new: the web app has no chemistry code, so there is **no TypeScript oracle**.
Tests use textbook / CODATA / IUPAC values instead (sources are in the comments of the test files).

## What is here
**Engine** (`lib/engine/chemistry/`, no Flutter imports):
- `elements.dart` (generated by `tools/gen_elements.py`), `element_types.dart`, `elements_lookup.dart`: all **118 elements**. Mass is the
  Appendix A number (so `molar` agrees with the table everywhere). Group, period, block, category, electron configuration (with
  superscripts), electronegativity, atomic radius, ionisation energy, electron affinity, oxidation states, standard state, melting and
  boiling point, density, year discovered and CPK colour come from the `mendeleev` 1.3.0 package at generation time. **A value the data
  does not have is `null` and shows as an em dash; nothing is invented.**
- `formula.dart`: formula parser (nested `()` `[]` `{}`, hydrates with `.` `·` `*` `•`, charges `^2-`, `2-`, `+3`, `Fe3+`, superscript digits,
  isotopes such as `13C` or `¹⁴C`, ASCII typing), molar mass, element breakdown, mass percent, empirical formula from percent or mass
  (ratios within 0.1 of a whole number, multipliers 1 to 10), moles / grams / particles, `prettyFormula` (typesets sub- and superscripts).
  Errors are `CalcError`s with a plain message (unknown element, unbalanced bracket, empty formula, bad charge, ...).
- `calc_functions.dart`: `molar("H2O")`, `amass("Fe")`, `atomic("Fe")`, `atomicsym(26)`. A pre-pass in `MathEvaluator.evaluate` resolves them
  **before** the infix `nCr` / `mod` regexes, so existing behaviour is unchanged for every expression without these names (the whole
  existing golden suite still passes). Quotes `"…"`, `“…”`, `'…'` are accepted.
- `solver_types.dart`, `solver_util.dart`: a small framework (`ChemSolver` → `ChemMode` → inputs / units / the formula text / `solve` returning
  numbers plus numbered working), so the screen builds every form from data.
- Solvers (`all_solvers.dart` lists them: 48 topics in 11 groups):
  - **Gas laws**: ideal gas (solve P, V, n, T), combined, Boyle, Charles, Gay-Lussac, Avogadro, Dalton, Graham, van der Waals.
  - **Solutions**: molarity, molality, normality, mole fraction, dilution, w/w, w/v, v/v, ppm, unit conversion, colligative properties.
  - **Acids & bases**: pH / pOH / [H+] / [OH-], strong acid or base (water term included), weak acid or base (exact quadratic, no x-small
    shortcut), buffer (Henderson-Hasselbalch), titration strong-strong and weak acid-strong base (before, at and after equivalence), Kw / pKa.
  - **Thermochemistry**: q = mcΔT, coffee-cup and bomb calorimetry, Hess's law (sum of up to 5 steps), ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, ΔG° ↔ K, phase-change heat.
  - **Kinetics**: zero / first / second order (concentration, time, k, half-life), Arrhenius (k, or Ea from two temperatures), rate law (rate, k).
  - **Equilibrium**: Kc ↔ Kp, ICE (exact, by monotone bisection on ln Q; any stoichiometry), reaction quotient and direction.
  - **Electrochemistry**: cell potential, Nernst, Faraday electrolysis, ΔG = −nFE.
  - **Stoichiometry**: **equation balancer** (exact integer / rational nullspace with a charge row, so ionic and redox equations work; reports
    "no balance", "more than one balance", "element on one side only", "wrong side"), moles / mass / particles, limiting reagent with
    leftover and theoretical yield, percent yield.
  - **Nuclear**: decay, activity, mass defect and binding energy (He-4 gives 28.296 MeV total).
  - **Quantum & atomic**: photon (E, λ, ν), Bohr levels and spectral lines, de Broglie (wavelength, speed, electron).
  - **Organic**: degrees of unsaturation, molecular formula from empirical formula and molar mass.
- `reference_tables.dart`: solubility rules, activity series, 33 polyatomic ions (each parses, with its charge in the text), pKa table,
  specific heats, molar masses of common compounds (computed from the element table), electronegativities (read from the element data).
- `chem_constants.dart`: 25 constants in 5 categories (gas constants in four units, standard conditions, Faraday, Kw, particle and
  atomic constants). CODATA 2018. Values the Physics Lab already has are taken from it, so the two screens cannot disagree.

**UI** (`lib/ui/screens/chemistry_*.dart`): a new optional mode, **Chemistry**, with six tabs.
- **Periodic Table**: 18-column grid with the two f-block rows below it (La–Lu, Ac–Lr, with the 57–71 / 89–103 markers), category colours and
  a legend that doubles as a filter, search by name / symbol / number, filters for category, block and state (non-matching cells dim), and a
  detail sheet with every field (electron configuration with real superscripts, oxidation states, K and °C, "Copy mass", "Use in calculator").
  Below about 34 px per cell (every phone) the grid scrolls sideways instead of shrinking.
- **Formula Tools**: formula → typeset formula, molar mass, breakdown table with mass percent; grams ↔ moles ↔ particles; empirical (and
  molecular) formula from percent or mass rows; degrees of unsaturation.
- **Solvers**: searchable list by group; each solver shows its relation (`PV = nRT`), labelled fields with units, "Solve", the answer, and the
  numbered working.
- **Balancer**: type an equation, get the balanced one with working; then limiting reagent, leftover, theoretical and percent yield.
- **Reference**: the seven tables above.
- **Constants**: search, categories, Copy and "Use in calculator" (same buttons as the Physics Lab).
- **Calculator**: a sixth Advanced tab, **Chemistry**, last in the strip, with `molar(`, `amass(`, `atomic(`, `atomicsym(`, a quote key, `( )`, comma,
  `N_A`, `R`, `k_B`, `q_e`, `atm`, and eight ready `molar("…")` formulas. The existing five tabs keep their order and keys.
- Wiring: `enableChemistry` setting (default **off**, "Features" switch in Settings), nav item after Physics Lab (bottom bar and rail), key `6`
  on a hardware keyboard, `enforceEnabledModes`, a hand-drawn flask nav icon (`NavIconId.chemistry`).

## Deliberate differences
1. **Chemistry is an optional mode, off by default**, like Physics Lab and the Grapher (Settings → Features). The master prompt asks for it to be
   reachable like the other tools; it is, once switched on. Say if you want it always on.
2. **Separate chemistry constants list.** The Physics Lab list is untouched (as required); the Chemistry tab has its own list that reuses the
   Physics values where they overlap.
3. **Isotope mass = mass number** (`13C` weighs 13 g/mol). The table has no isotope masses and none were invented; `ChemFormula.hasIsotopes`
   makes the screen say so.
4. **`Fe3+` reads as Fe with charge +3** (digit before the sign is the charge); `Fe+3` and `Fe^3+` mean the same. `O2-` is the oxide ion (charge
   −2), not O₂; write `O2` for the molecule.
5. **`atomicsym(n)` works as a whole expression** (its answer is text). Inside a longer expression it is an error, because the evaluator is
   numeric. `atomic("Fe")` and `amass("Fe")` are numbers and work anywhere, e.g. `2*amass("Fe")`.
6. **Element detail is a dialog**, not the `M3Sheet`: `M3Sheet` lives in the shell's overlay stack, a screen cannot open it.
7. **Tabs do not keep their fields** when you switch tab (the Physics Lab does not either).
8. The balancer ignores coefficients you type and always finds the smallest whole numbers.
9. Flask nav icon is drawn by hand (outline like lucide `flask-conical`); `lucide-react` is not installed here, so the generator was not extended.

## Not done / open
- No screenshot goldens and no device check. Nothing in the periodic-table layout was seen on a phone.
- Data is from one source (`mendeleev`) plus Appendix A masses; I did not cross-check the other 17 fields element by element against a second
  reference. What is tested: 118 entries, Z = 1..118 in order, unique symbols and names, anchor values from Appendix A, period / group / block layout, nulls stay null, lookup and search.
- ICE handles one equilibrium step with up to two reactants and two products as input fields; multi-step coupled equilibria are not offered.
- The solvers use 25 °C Kw = 1.0e-14 (the textbook value) and say so in their working.

## Parity doubts
1. **Bottom bar at 360 px.** In the test harness the bottom navigation overflows by 14 px at a 360 px window, also with only Physics Lab on (so it is
   not caused by Chemistry). With Physics, Chemistry and the Grapher all on, the bar has seven items; I did not look at that on a device.
2. **Electron configurations** are the ground states from the package; for the known irregular ones (Cr, Cu, Pd, the f-block) I trust the package and
   did not re-derive them.
3. **Colour of the category cells** is computed from fixed hues; contrast on the AMOLED and dynamic themes is by reasoning, not by eye.
4. **Weak-acid titration at the equivalence point** uses the conjugate-base quadratic, the way textbooks do; some courses use the approximation.
