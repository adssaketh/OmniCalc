OmniCalc Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 October 2026

OmniCalc is a free, open-source calculator suite for Android: a scientific calculator, unit converter, physics constants, finance tools and currency converter. It is developed by Saketh (adsaketh@gmail.com). This page explains, in plain words, what OmniCalc can see and what it does with it.

The short version

  • OmniCalc does not collect, sell or share your personal information.
  • There is no account, no sign-in and no profile on a server.
  • There are no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no crash-reporting service.
  • Your calculations, history and settings stay on your phone.
  • The only time OmniCalc uses the internet is to download public currency exchange rates when you use the currency converter. It sends none of your data with that request.
  • OmniCalc asks only for Android's standard internet permission. It does not ask for your camera, microphone, contacts, location, messages, photos or files.

What OmniCalc stores on your device

OmniCalc keeps a few things in its own private storage on your phone, so the app remembers where you left off:

  • your calculation history (the expressions you evaluated and their results),
  • your settings, such as theme, angle mode (degrees or radians), decimal precision, sound and haptic choices, and the bar and scroll-edge options,
  • the exchange rates last downloaded, so the currency converter still works offline,
  • a note of whether you chose a theme yourself.

Other apps cannot read this storage. None of it is uploaded by OmniCalc.

Internet use: currency exchange rates

When you open the currency converter (and when your phone reconnects to the internet while it is open, or you tap Refresh), OmniCalc downloads a table of current exchange rates from public services:

  • open.er-api.com
  • api.exchangerate-api.com
  • the open currency-api data files served by jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) and by Cloudflare Pages (latest.currency-api.pages.dev)

These are ordinary web requests for a public file. As with any website, the service that answers can see your phone's IP address and basic technical details such as the type of browser engine. OmniCalc does not send your calculations, amounts, chosen currencies, history or any identifier. Each of these services has its own privacy policy. If you never open the currency converter, OmniCalc makes no network requests. If you are offline, it uses the last saved rates, or built-in approximate ones. Exchange rates are indicative only and should not be used for financial decisions without checking a bank or broker.

Sounds, vibration and the clipboard

  • Key-click sounds are generated on your phone while you use the app. Nothing is recorded and no microphone is used.
  • When you tap a copy button, OmniCalc puts the text you chose (for example a result) on the clipboard.
  • OmniCalc reads the clipboard only when you paste into it yourself.

Android backup

OmniCalc's saved data (history, settings and cached exchange rates) may be included in Android's automatic backup to your Google account, if you have device backup turned on. That is a feature of Android and your Google account, controlled by you in your phone's settings. The developer of OmniCalc receives nothing from it. You can turn it off in Android's backup settings.

Links that open outside the app

"Source code on GitHub" and the standalone privacy policy open in your web browser. When you follow them, the website you visit, such as GitHub, applies its own privacy policy.

Your choices

  • Clear the history tape in the app to delete your calculation history.
  • Use Reset in Settings to restore the default settings.
  • Clear OmniCalc's storage in Android settings, or uninstall the app, to remove everything it stored.
  • Turn off Android backup if you do not want your data included in it.

Children

OmniCalc does not collect personal information from anyone, including children. It has no accounts, ads or purchases.

Open source

OmniCalc is free software under the GNU General Public License v3.0 (GPL-3.0). The code is public at https://github.com/adssaketh/OmniCalc, so anyone can check that it does what this policy says.

Changes to this policy

If OmniCalc's handling of data ever changes, this page will be updated and the date at the top will change. OmniCalc will not start collecting data without telling you here first.
